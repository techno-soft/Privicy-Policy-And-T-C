## 3. Information stored on your device

The app stores the following **locally on your device only**:

- **Scan history** — the content of codes you scan, their type and the time
  they were scanned.
- **Created codes** — the content and styling of codes you generate,
  including any logo image you add.
- **Settings** — your preferences, such as theme, language, and whether
  sound and vibration are on.
- **Subscription status** — a single flag recording whether your
  subscription is active, so the app works offline.

This data is held in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot
read. It is never uploaded.

**You control it.** You can delete individual entries, clear all history at
once, or set history to delete itself automatically after 30, 90 or 365
days — all in Settings. Deleting the app removes everything.

Note that if you have device backup enabled (iCloud Backup or Android
Backup), your device operating system may include the app's local data in
your own backup. That backup is controlled by Apple or Google under their
own privacy policies, not by us, and we have no access to it.

## 4. Permissions and why we need them

| Permission | Why | What happens to the data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Camera** | To read codes | Frames are analysed on your device in real time and are never recorded, saved or transmitted |
| **Photo library (read)** | To scan a code from a picture you choose | The image is analysed on your device; we do not copy or upload it |
| **Photo library (add)** | To save a code you created | Only the image you ask to save is written |
| **Vibration** | Haptic feedback when a scan succeeds | No data involved |
| **Internet** | Required by the app store billing library, and to open a link you tap after scanning it | No data is sent to us, because we run no server |

You can refuse or revoke any permission in your device settings. Refusing
camera access disables scanning; the rest of the app continues to work.

## 5. Links in scanned codes

A QR code can contain a link to any website. When you scan one, the app
shows you the full address and warns you if it looks suspicious — a
shortened link, an address using characters that imitate another site, or an
unencrypted connection.

**Opening a link is always your choice.** By default the app never opens a
link automatically.

Once you open a link, you leave our app and the website's own privacy policy
applies. We have no control over, and take no responsibility for, the
content or privacy practices of any site reached through a scanned code.

## 7. Third-party components

The app is built with open-source software libraries that run entirely on
your device. Relevant here:

- **Google ML Kit (Barcode Scanning)** — the on-device model that detects
  codes in camera frames. It runs locally and does not send images to
  Google.
- **Apple AVFoundation / Vision** — the equivalent on iOS, also fully
  on-device.
- **Apple StoreKit / Google Play Billing** — subscription handling, as
  described above.

We do not include any advertising network, analytics SDK, crash reporter or
tracking library.

A full list of open-source components is available in the app under
**Settings → Open source licences**.
